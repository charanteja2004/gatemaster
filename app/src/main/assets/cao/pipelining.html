Pipelining

Overlap instructions by splitting execution into stages, so a new instruction starts each cycle while earlier ones finish.

Cycles for n instructions in a k-stage pipeline = k + (n − 1)

Speedup = n·k / (k + n − 1) → k as n grows

Clock period = max stage delay + latch delay

A 100 ns non-pipelined instruction split into 5 equal stages with 2 ns latches gives a 22 ns clock and a speedup of 100/22 ≈ 4.55, short of the ideal 5.

Speedup is limited by the slowest stage, not the average. Balancing stages matters more than shortening any one of them.

Hazards break the one-per-cycle rate: structural (a resource clash), data (an operand not ready) and control (a branch not yet resolved).
