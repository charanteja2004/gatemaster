Priority Scheduling

Each process carries a priority; the scheduler picks the highest. It exists in preemptive and non-preemptive forms.

Low-priority processes can starve indefinitely. Ageing raises a waiting process's priority over time and fixes it.

Priority inversion: a high-priority task blocks on a lock held by a low-priority task, which is itself preempted by a medium-priority task. Priority inheritance — briefly raising the holder's priority — is the standard fix.

SJF is priority scheduling where the priority is the inverse of the next burst.
