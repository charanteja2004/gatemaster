Alphanumeric Series

  1. Take first differences; if they are constant, the series is linear.
  2. If the differences themselves form a pattern, take second differences.
  3. Check ratios for a geometric series.
  4. Check squares, cubes, primes, and n² ± n.

2, 6, 12, 20, 30 has differences 4, 6, 8, 10, so the next term is 42. Equivalently the nth term is n(n+1).

3, 7, 15, 31, 63 doubles and adds one each time, giving 127 — the terms are 2ⁿ − 1.

Letter series

  • Write letter positions as numbers: A = 1 … Z = 26.
  • Opposite letters sum to 27: A ↔ Z, B ↔ Y, C ↔ X.

AZ, BY, CX continues DW — the first letter advances while the second is its mirror.
