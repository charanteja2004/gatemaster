Inequality Reasoning

A chain of relations, followed by conclusions to accept or reject.

  • A chain combines only when every link points the same way.
  • A chain with at least one strict link gives a strict conclusion.
  • A chain of all ≥ gives only ≥, never >.

A > B ≥ C > D gives A > D. But M ≥ N > O ≤ P gives M > O and nothing at all about M against P, because the direction reverses at O.

Equality is transparent: in P ≥ Q = R, substitute R for Q freely, so P ≥ R follows.

Either-or conclusions are true when the two together cover every case and neither holds alone — typically a pair like X ≥ Y and X < Y.
